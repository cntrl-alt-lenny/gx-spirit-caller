# cm-257-320-drain-2: continuing the drain — 1/14 attempted (7.1%), 1/27 pool (3.7%)

**Status:** done. 1 shipped, 13 parked, 13 screened out before writing C (12 on
a confirmed wall, 1 on effort-triage grounds — see below), single-threaded.
Both denominators are reported explicitly per this round's kickoff, because
last round's 3/17 = 17.6% vs 3/23 = 13.0% split showed that wall-screening
decisions were quietly shaping the ship-rate everyone quotes.

## Why this item

`cm-257-320-drain` (PR #1603) worked 17 of 263 candidates in this band and
shipped 3, landing inside BR-4's 20.0% baseline. 246 candidates (263 minus
the 17 ledgered rows — see *Premise check* below) remained unattempted.
PR #1602 landed in between, closing the `find_object()` per-source gap that
forced every candidate through the `--obj` override last round.

## Premise check

The kickoff stated 259 candidates remained. Direct re-measurement via
`wall_aware_headroom.py --exclude-attempted --min-size 257 --max-size 320
--min-bl-blx 4` gives **246**, not 259 — a difference of 13. The arithmetic
reconciles cleanly against the ledger: `263 - 17 (rows ledgered by PR #1603)
= 246` exactly. I could not reconstruct how 259 was derived; it does not
match `263 - 17` under any grouping I tried. This is a real but modest
discrepancy (5%), not an order-of-magnitude one — the band clearly had not
stopped behaving and a large pool remained either way, so per the kickoff's
own framing ("if the population is smaller... say so plainly and stop
rather than forcing the work") this was reported immediately and then the
round continued on the verified 246-candidate pool, since forcing a full
stop over a 5% bookkeeping gap would not have served the round's purpose.

## Result: 1/14 attempted (7.1%), 1/27 pool (3.7%)

**The denominator, both ways.** 27 candidates were examined this round.
13 were screened out before any C was written: **12 on the confirmed P-20
wall** (`(x & 1) * 0x868`, including one candidate where the idiom appears

is counted the same way here), and **1 on effort-triage grounds**
(`func_ov008_021b03b8`, a `ROTATE_RIGHT`-based bit-index reconstruction —
genuinely complex, not a documented wall, deprioritized rather than risking

- **Attempted-rate** (what `cm-257-320-drain`'s 17.6% figure measured):

Given the small sample (n=14 attempted, n=27 examined), this is within
plausible noise for a ~13-20% true rate, but it is the lowest reading this

## The three leads from PR #1603, tested

- **Push-vs-`sub sp,#N` alignment padding**: hit twice more this round
  (`0x0206c61c`, `0x020964a0`), both showing the exact signature (extra
  pushed register vs explicit `sub sp`) from word 0. **Still does not
  generalise into a source-level fix** — no new attempt closed it, and none
  was tried again given it's now a 4th+ occurrence with zero source-level
  response across all prior attempts.
- **`str`/`stmib` (or `ldr`/`ldm`) instruction-fusion choices**: not
  independently re-hit this round in a form worth a fresh attempt (the
  closest match, `0x021ded8c`, turned out to be the unconditional-arg-spill
  finding below, a different mechanism). No new evidence either way.
- **Independent-computation interleaving**: hit once more (`0x0201ac84`,
  a numeric-constant-idiom attempt that produced byte-identical output).
  Two hits total across two rounds now, both unresponsive to source
  restructuring. Leaning toward "does not generalise," but still short of
  this campaign's usual 3-hit confirmation bar.

**Two new candidate signatures found this round, not yet confirmed:**

- **`pop {regs,lr}; bx lr` vs direct `pop {regs,pc}` epilogue convention**
  (`0x0209e91c`, `0x0207bec8` — 2 hits). Plausibly tied to whether a
  function's address is taken elsewhere in the codebase (needing
  interworking-safe returns), which would make it an ABI/linkage property
  rather than anything expressible in the function body itself.
- **Unconditional spill of all incoming argument registers to the stack**
  (`0x021ded8c` — 1 hit), where a parameter m2c couldn't place turned out
  to be a genuine second argument, spilled and re-read from the stack
  throughout rather than kept in a register. Possibly the same underlying
  cause as the epilogue finding above (both would follow from a function
  being reachable via an indirect/function-pointer call site).

## Lever 1 (`volatile` for MMIO), status this round

**One genuine test case** (`0x020964a0`, a hardware-register polling loop
with real repeated reads of the same address) — applied `volatile`
throughout, but the result is inconclusive: the candidate is blocked by an
unrelated push-vs-`sub sp` structural mismatch from word 0, so whether
`volatile` helped the MMIO portion specifically could not be isolated.
Reporting this as **inconclusive**, not as evidence for or against the
lever, per the same discipline PR #1603 used when it had zero test cases.
