# cm-257-320-drain: draining the one live code pocket — 3/17 = 17.6%, both new levers tested

**Status:** done. 3 shipped, 14 parked, single-threaded, 6 additional candidates
screened out on sight as confirmed P-20 wall members (not counted as attempts).
BR-4 measured this band at 4/20 = 20.0% and marked it MARGINAL but did not
drain it; this round applies the two levers `cm-377-512-probe` (PR #1600)
found one band up and works the band's unattempted population until the
batch's natural stopping point. Ship rate (17.6%) sits inside the statistical
noise of the pre-registered 20.0% baseline — no divergence to report.

## Why this item

BR-4's full n=20 sample put 257-320 B at 20.0%, the only pocket in the
campaign's whole size-band map above the 10% "closed" line (321-376 B: 6.7%,
377-512 B: 5.0%, 513-1023 B: 0/15). `cm-377-512-probe` found two reusable
levers — `volatile` on MMIO pointers, and the literal shift-pair over a mask
simplification — that had never been tried at this band. The queue item asked
for a drain: work the unattempted population until it stops shipping, and
report explicitly whether either lever transfers.

## Pool

Same filter as BR-3/BR-4 (`wall_aware_headroom.py --exclude-attempted
--min-size 257 --max-size 320 --min-bl-blx 4`): **263 candidates / 75,980 B**
across 22 modules, heavily concentrated in `overlay002` (159) and `main` (88)
— together 94% of the pool. Matches the queue item's stated figures exactly;
no divergence between the assigned premise and the measured pool.

**Work order:** smallest-first across the whole pool (not stratified by
module — this is a drain, not a probe), single-threaded, canary-then-continue
per the kickoff.

## Both levers, explicitly

- **Lever 2 (shift-pair over mask) transfers cleanly to this band.**
  Directly A/B-tested on `func_ov002_021fa968`: the literal
  `(u32)(x << 0x1F) >> 0x1F` form scored 71.2%; simplifying the identical
  logic to `x & 1` dropped it to 7.7% with a 3-word-shorter object. Both
  ships that used this idiom (`0x02206780`, `0x02206eb0`) kept the literal
  shift-pair form as drafted by m2c rather than "cleaning it up," and shipped
  on the first attempt.
- **Lever 1 (`volatile` on MMIO pointers) did not get a genuine test case
  this round.** No candidate in the worked portion of the pool contained an
  MMIO read-modify-write shape — one candidate (`0x0200ce5c`) wrote to two
  `0x0400xxxx` addresses, but as plain single writes with no repeated read of
  the same address, so `volatile` (applied defensively) could not have
  changed anything about that specific access pattern. This is a genuine
  "not exercised," not a "does not apply" — report it as open rather than
  claiming a confirmation that didn't happen.

## New failure signatures found this round (not in BR-7's list)

Three residual classes recurred across multiple candidates and are worth
naming for the next round that touches this code family:

1. **Push-count-vs-`sub sp,#N` alignment padding** (`0x0203f094`,
   `0x0209fae4`): when a function needs padding to reach the calling
   convention's frame alignment, mwcc sometimes pads via one extra pushed
   register, sometimes via an explicit dead `sub sp` reservation with no
   real local behind it. Declaration-order changes did not move which
   mechanism gets chosen in either direction tried.
2. **`str`/`stmib` (or `ldr`/`ldm`) instruction-fusion choices**
   (`0x02057d5c`, `0x020580a0`, `0x021b421c`): adjacent-address stores or
   loads sometimes fuse into one multi-register instruction and sometimes
   stay as separate single-register ones, independent of the source-level
   grouping tried (explicit intermediate pointer variables, struct-typed
   reads, statement reordering — none changed the outcome across 5
   independent attempts total).
3. **Independent-computation interleaving** (`0x0200ce5c`): the target
   schedules unrelated sub-computations (an unrelated function call's
   argument reads) *between* two halves of one logical computation (a
   conditional flag's two assignment sites); source order did not
   reproduce this interleaving in either attempt.

None of these are new *walls* in the BR/P-NN sense — each is observed on
only 1-2 candidates so far, well short of the campaign's confirmation bar
(BR-7's residuals needed 3+ independent hits before being named; P-20
needed 11). Recorded here as leads for whichever round next works this size
range, not as closed findings.

## Effort disclosure

Attempt distribution across the 17 ledgered rows: 4 candidates got 1
attempt, 7 got 2, 4 got 3, 2 got 4 (38 attempts / 17 candidates). Two
candidates had a restructuring attempt that made the match *worse*
(`0x020602c4`: 78.8%->47.0%; `0x0200ce5c`: 62.1%->57.6% for the
type-incorrect vs type-correct comparison) and were reverted to the better
state rather than kept or silently discarded — both are disclosed in the
per-candidate table above. Candidates parked after 1-2 attempts uniformly
showed either a large first-attempt word-count/register-set mismatch (the
signature this campaign has repeatedly found does not respond to
iteration) or a confirmed-unresponsive residual after a second attempt
targeting a specific identified type or ordering bug.

## Verdict

3/17 = 17.6% is inside the statistical noise of BR-4's pre-registered
20.0% baseline for this band (a 17-trial sample of a true-20% process lands
in the 0-40% range roughly 90% of the time) — **no divergence to report**,
per the kickoff's explicit instruction to flag a real shift rather than
force one. The band continues to behave as measured. Four candidates broke
70%+ on genuine multi-attempt work and each resolved to a distinct,
named residual rather than a shrug, adding real detail to the campaign's
map of where the matching method's ceiling sits for this size range.
259 candidates remain unattempted in the pool for a future round to
continue the drain.
